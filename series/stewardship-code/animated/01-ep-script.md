# EPISODE 1 — THE OWNERSHIP CODE
## *God owns it; I manage it* · Luke 16:1–2 · Psalm 24:1

Target runtime **4:05**. Draws on shorts 02–07.
Narration budget: **738 characters** (counted below — ElevenLabs is the constraint).

The episode turns on one object: a phone Micah believes is his.

## Script

### 1 · COLD OPEN — 0:00–0:30

> **VISUAL** — Black. One sound: a phone hitting tile. Hard cut to a cracked screen face-up
> on bathroom tiles, spiderweb fracture spreading across a lit display. Gold light bleeds
> from the crack. Micah's hand enters and picks it up, slowly. Reflection of his eye in the
> broken glass.

**MICAH** *(flat, winded)*
> Three months. Three months of washing cars for this.

> **VISUAL** — School corridor. Ada falls into step beside him. He doesn't look up.

**ADA**
> Ouch. Is it dead?

**MICAH**
> It still works. That's not the point.

**ADA**
> So what is the point?

**MICAH**
> It was mine, Ada. I earned it. And now it's ruined.

> **VISUAL** — Push in on the crack. The gold light through it flares — hold one beat too
> long, so it feels like the object is looking back at him.

### 2 · TITLE — 0:30–0:38

> **VISUAL** — Navy. THE STEWARDSHIP CODE assembles per letter, gold sweep across it.
> Beneath, a vault ring turns one click and seats: **THE OWNERSHIP CODE**.

### 3 · COMPLICATION — 0:38–1:45

> **VISUAL** — Mr Okafor's electronics shop. Warm practical light, shelves of parts, a
> soldering iron breathing smoke. Okafor turns the cracked phone over in both hands.

**MR OKAFOR**
> Screen replacement. Twelve thousand.

**MICAH**
> I don't have twelve thousand. I had twelve thousand. It's in this phone.

**MR OKAFOR** *(not unkind)*
> Mm. Tell me how you bought it.

**MICAH**
> I washed cars. Every Saturday, all through the holiday. Forty-one cars.

**MR OKAFOR**
> Good. And the water?

**MICAH**
> …The water?

**MR OKAFOR**
> The water you washed them with. Did you make it?

> **VISUAL** — Micah's face. The question hasn't landed yet; it has only annoyed him.

**MICAH**
> It's from the tap.

**MR OKAFOR**
> And the arms.

> **VISUAL** — Micah looks down at his own hands, palms up, still faintly stained with
> polish. Gold rim light along the fingers.

**MR OKAFOR** *(quieter)*
> Forty-one cars is real work. I am not taking it from you. I am asking who gave you the
> strength for the forty-first one.

> **VISUAL** — Silence in the shop. Only the soldering iron hissing. Micah says nothing.

### 4 · THE TURN — 1:45–2:25

> **VISUAL** — Okafor slides a drawer open, takes out a slim paper receipt, and puts it on
> the counter between them. Extreme close-up: blank paper, no printing. Gold light across it.

**MR OKAFOR**
> Every owner can show a receipt. Where is yours?

> **VISUAL** — Micah reaches for it. His fingers stop just short. The paper stays blank.
>
> Cut wide and up: the shop, the street, the city, then the coastline, then the curve of the
> earth in gold light — a single continuous pull-back, no cuts.

**NARRATOR**
> The earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof.

**NARRATOR**
> He is not a partner in the economy. He is the Owner. And there has never been a second name
> on the deed.

### 5 · THE OWNERSHIP CODE — 2:25–3:15

> **VISUAL** — The vault chamber from the trailer. One vast ring turning, meander patterns
> along its face. Micah stands small before it. Ada steps in beside him.

**NARRATOR**
> Stewardship begins the moment you stop behaving like an absolute owner.

> **VISUAL** — Ada holds out a laptop, offering it. Clean insert of Micah's hands receiving
> it carefully, thumbs on the edges, the way you hold something borrowed.

**ADA**
> When I lent you my brother's laptop, you carried it in two hands. You put it down slowly.
> You didn't eat near it.

**MICAH**
> Because it wasn't mine.

**ADA**
> Right. So why do you handle your own life more carelessly than a laptop you borrowed for a
> week?

> **VISUAL** — Two gold discs on Okafor's counter, spinning. One slows and settles flat. The
> other keeps turning.

**NARRATOR**
> There are only two positions available to you. Owner, or manager. One of them is already
> taken.

### 6 · DECISION — 3:15–3:45

> **VISUAL** — Micah's room, night. Desk lamp. He tears a page from a notebook and writes a
> heading by hand — we see only the pen, the paper, and the movement, never legible text.
> Beside him, the cracked phone, face up, still working.

**MICAH** *(to himself, steady)*
> What God owns, but has placed in my hands.

> **VISUAL** — Seven quick inserts, each a half-second, gold light streaking between:
> the phone · his hands · a clock face · a football boot · a schoolbook · his mother asleep
> in a chair · the window, and the city beyond it.

**MICAH**
> Number one. This phone. Cracked. Still working. Still not mine.

> **VISUAL** — He sets the pen down. He looks at the phone differently — not with regret.

### 7 · CHALLENGE + END CARD — 3:45–4:05

**NARRATOR**
> This week, make that list. Seven things, minimum. Time, money, devices, skills,
> opportunities. Then handle one of them the way you'd handle something borrowed.

> **VISUAL** — Rooftop, dawn. Micah and Ada in silhouette against a gold sunrise over the
> city. He raises the cracked phone; the light comes through the fracture and throws gold
> across both of them.

**MICAH**
> Christ my Master. Money my servant.

> **VISUAL** — Cut to navy. Title lockup with gold sweep. Beneath: *The Ownership Code.*
> KIN mark shimmers and resolves. **SUBSCRIBE ON ALL PLATFORMS · @KidsInspiringNation**

## Production notes

**The blank receipt is the episode.** It is the one image that has to work: an owner can
produce a receipt, Micah cannot, and the paper stays empty when he reaches for it. Generate
it as a clean insert — paper, hands, gold light, no printing of any kind. If Flow puts a
single character of text on that paper the shot is dead and gets re-rolled.

**Never show legible writing.** Micah's list is shot as pen, hand and movement only. The
negative prompt bans text for good reason, and the heading is spoken instead so we don't need
it.

**Okafor must not become the villain.** "Forty-one cars is real work. I am not taking it from
you." He affirms the labour and then asks the harder question. The episode fails if it reads
as an adult belittling a teenager's effort — the whole point is that Micah's work was real
*and* it was never the whole story.

**The pull-back in beat 4 is one continuous move**, shop to orbit. It is the most expensive
idea in the episode and probably needs two generations stitched with a whip-pan or a light
flare on the seam. Budget for that.
